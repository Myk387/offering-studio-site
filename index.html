Why it matters

The book is the first thing an owner judges you on.

On a $6M apartment building, the owner is choosing between you and a national brokerage with a twelve-person marketing floor. They see two proposals. One is a bound, typeset book with real comps, a clean submarket map, and a setup sheet that mirrors their accountant's. The other came out of a template.

That gap is not talent. It is production capacity — and it is the single cheapest thing on the deal to fix. A $1,500 book on a listing that pays $90,000 is rounding error.

Not a template filler

Software drops your numbers into a stock layout. We typeset the deal — page architecture built around what this asset's story actually is.

Not a graphic designer

A designer makes it pretty and asks you what a T-12 is. We read the model, the rent roll, and the prelim, and the book reconciles to all three.

The difference

We read the model.

Most of what goes wrong in an OM is not design. It is a cap rate on page 4 that disagrees with the setup sheet on page 12, and a buyer's analyst catching it.

Reconciled

Every figure traces to a source

Price, cap, GRM, NOI, and returns match your model exactly. $/unit and $/SF are re-derived from the sale price rather than retyped. Where two sources disagree, we surface it instead of silently picking one.

Disclosed

Material items get said out loud

Rent control, recorded notices, pending litigation, tax default, easements. A book that buries what the prelim shows is a liability, not a marketing document.

Nothing invented

No fabricated numbers, ever

If a rent, an expense line, or a comp cannot be sourced, it is flagged for you to fill — never guessed at to make a page look complete. These are regulated documents.

Questions

Before you send the first deal.

Whose branding goes on the book?

Yours. Your logo, your palette, your contact block, your disclaimers. We are a production shop, not a co-brand — nothing on the finished document says Offering Studio. The one-time house format setup exists so that every future book comes out already matching your identity.

What exactly do you need from me to start?

Your underwriting model or pricing sheet, the rent roll, the property facts (MLS sheet, assessor data, or just an email describing it), and any photos. If you have a prelim title report, send it — material disclosures belong in the book. Missing comps we can source; missing financials we cannot invent.

Do you handle the photography?

No. We work with what you provide — listing photos, drone stills, renderings. If a deal has no usable photography we will tell you before the draft rather than pad the book with stock imagery, and we can recommend a shooter in your market.

Is my deal information confidential?

Yes. Rent rolls, models, and owner information are never published, never used as portfolio samples, and never shared. The samples on this site are built on entirely fictional properties for exactly this reason. A mutual NDA before the first file is welcome.

Who is actually doing the work?

An active commercial brokerage professional who has produced these books for institutional multifamily and development listings, working with a purpose-built production pipeline. That is why the setup sheet is right and the comps reconcile — this is not outsourced page layout.

What if I don't like the draft?

Revisions are unlimited and usually same-day. If the first draft misses badly enough that you would rather walk, say so within 48 hours of receiving it and there is no fee. That has to be worth more to us than one invoice.

Can you work in my existing template?

Sometimes. If you have a PowerPoint or InDesign house format you are committed to, send it and we will tell you honestly whether we can match it or whether you are better served by a rebuild. We will not pretend a weak template is fine.

Do you work with agents at national brokerages?

Not currently. The service is built for independent and boutique shops without an in-house marketing department. Agents at national firms already have one.
