Pricing logic

Plan A carries the highest per-foot price and the lowest absolute price — the standard relationship in attached product, where the smallest plan reaches the deepest buyer pool and clears first. Plan C is priced at a discount per foot to its size because four-bedroom attached product in this submarket has a materially thinner buyer pool, and the two units are positioned to close last.

Sensitivity worth testing

Hard cost is the dominant variable. A $20 per foot move — roughly 7% — swings profit by $462,000 and the margin by 3.5 points. A buyer should price this deal against their own subcontractor bids rather than the $290 assumption used here. Selling costs assume a 5% total load; a developer with an in-house sales team will underwrite lower. Every figure on this slide is invented for this sample.

On the 0.9% premium

The comparable set is unusually tight — a seven-dollar per foot spread across five sales — which makes the projection defensible but also caps it. We have modeled a 0.9% premium to the average on the basis of 2028 delivery against a 2023–2025 comparable set, and nothing more. A buyer underwriting meaningful price appreciation over the construction period is taking a view this document does not take. Every figure here is invented for this sample.

Absorption assumption

The model assumes 2.0 closings per month across a seven-month sales run, consistent with the comparable projects in the closed-sale set. At that pace the fourteen units clear inside the twenty-four month schedule with a month of slack. A slower two-year market would extend the carry rather than break the deal — the sensitivity that matters here is cost, not absorption.

Financing note

The budget is presented unlevered. A construction facility at 65% loan to cost would carry roughly $6.9 million with interest capitalized, and is not reflected in the 19.1% return on cost shown on slide 5 — a levered buyer will underwrite a materially higher equity return and a materially higher risk profile. Every figure in this document is invented for this sample.
