Ridgeway Gardens Apartments does not exist. The address, ownership, brokerage, agents, license numbers, rents, expenses, comparables, and every financial figure shown were invented to demonstrate document structure. Nothing here describes a real property, a real opinion of value, or a real engagement.

Purpose. This Broker Opinion of Value has been prepared at the request of ownership to assist in evaluating a potential disposition. It sets out an opinion of the price the subject would most probably achieve in an arm's-length sale, exposed to the open market for a reasonable period.

This is not an appraisal. It has not been prepared in conformance with the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice, and the preparers are licensed real estate brokers rather than certified appraisers. It may not be relied upon for lending, litigation, taxation, estate, or regulatory purposes. Where a certified valuation is required, ownership should engage a licensed appraiser.

Information relied upon. The opinion rests on a rent roll and trailing operating statements supplied by ownership, public assessor records, and comparable transaction data obtained from third-party sources. None of this material has been independently audited or verified. Should the rent roll, in-place rents, or operating expenses differ materially from what was supplied, the conclusion will change.

Rent regulation. The subject sits within a jurisdiction subject to local rent-stabilization regulation. Allowable increases, relocation obligations, and just-cause eviction requirements materially constrain the pace at which the rent upside identified here can be realized. Ownership should confirm the regulatory status of each unit with counsel.

Market conditions. The conclusion reflects conditions as of the date of preparation. Interest rates, credit availability, and buyer appetite in this asset class move quickly; an opinion more than ninety days old should be refreshed before it is acted upon.

No obligation. Delivery of this document does not create an agency relationship or a listing agreement, and imposes no obligation on either party.

Where we would start

We recommend listing at $7,450,000 rather than at the midpoint of the range. It prices just above the comparable per-unit average — justified by the subject's 1986 vintage and two-bathroom plans — while remaining inside the cap band every recent buyer has underwritten to. Reading the matrix

Buyers in this size band underwrite primarily to going-in cap rate and secondarily to price per unit. The two metrics disagree slightly here: at $7,450,000 the subject is 0.5% above the comparable average per unit but 5 basis points wide of the average cap — meaning it looks marginally expensive on a unit basis and marginally cheap on yield. That is a favorable place to be, because the yield argument is the one a lender and an investment committee test.

Marketing Process
13 Weeks to Close

A structured process with a fixed offer date. The objective is to create a single moment of competition rather than a sequence of one-off negotiations.

Weeks 1–2

Preparation

  • Professional photography and drone
  • Offering memorandum produced
  • Financial package assembled and reconciled to the trailing twelve
  • Title ordered and reviewed for disclosure items
  • Tenant notification plan agreed with ownership
Weeks 3–5

Active Marketing

  • Launch to our buyer database and the major listing platforms
  • Direct outreach to 1031 exchange buyers in escrow
  • Broker-community distribution across the Valley
  • Scheduled property tours, twice weekly
  • Weekly written activity report to ownership
Week 6

Call for Offers

  • Fixed offer deadline announced at launch
  • Offers summarized side by side on price, terms, and buyer credibility
  • Best and final round with shortlisted buyers
  • Ownership selects; letter of intent executed
Weeks 7–13

Contract & Close

  • Purchase agreement negotiated and signed
  • 21-day due diligence with deposit released at expiration
  • Appraisal and lender coordination
  • 30-day close following due diligence
  • Estoppels, rent roll certification, and transfer of deposits

What we need from ownership

Certified rent roll and trailing-twelve operating statements  ·  copies of all leases and any written rent-increase notices  ·  service contracts and the laundry lease  ·  most recent tax bill and insurance declaration  ·  any permits or plans for work performed since 1998  ·  access for photography and scheduled tours with proper tenant notice.

On the offer deadline

A fixed offer date is the single most effective mechanism available to a seller in this size band. It converts a sequence of individual negotiations, in which each buyer knows only their own position, into one round in which every buyer must assume they are competing. In our experience it is worth more than any amount of individual negotiation after the fact — and it is only credible if the launch price sits inside the range buyers can verify, which is the argument made on page 5.
